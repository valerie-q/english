💡 Past tense tips

When telling a story in English, mix three past tenses to make it natural:

Past Simple — main events I dropped my phone. She called me. We ran away. → use for actions in order
Past Continuous — background It was raining. I was walking home. She was waiting. → use for what was happening around the event
Past Perfect — before the story I had forgotten my bag. She had already left. → use for what happened earlier

⚠️ Stay in the past — don't switch to present when the story gets exciting!

Grammar Bank 1D — except for, apart from, (not) even
except for

We use except for + noun to say that something is not included.

The school teaches most ball sports except for golf.

We use except for to introduce the one person/thing that means a statement isn't 100% true.

All the children are here except for Leila.
I like vegetables, except for onions.

When except for follows a noun, we can omit for.

She loves ball games except (for) golf.
They speak all the Latin-based languages except (for) Romanian.

When except joins two clauses, it cannot be followed by for.
My dogs are similar except the oldest one is bigger.

apart from

Apart from + noun means the same as except for.

We're open every day apart from Sunday.
Apart from Kenzo, the whole family came to the wedding.
I like all types of films apart from documentaries.
Apart from the sound of the clocks, the house was silent.

even

We use even to emphasise that something is surprising or unusual.

John doesn't usually watch TV, but even he liked that programme!
I'll help you with the project. I'll even stay with you after work to finish it.

not even

We use not even to emphasise a surprising negative point.

Not even Jo, whose English is very good, could understand what he was saying.
She'd changed so much I didn't even recognise her!
