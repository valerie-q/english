Grammar Reference — Giving Instructions (Imperative) BP p.106
3.2 — Giving Instructions

We use the imperative to give instructions or orders to one person or a group of people.

Turn on your computer, then enter the password.
Go to reception and complete a form.

We can add please to be more polite:

Please leave by 6.00 p.m.     Please don't move the boxes.

Positive

Use the infinitive verb form (e.g. remove, not removes). Do not use subject pronouns (e.g. you, they).

+ Come to the office.  You come to the office.
+Remove everything from your desk.
+Come to the office at 10 o'clock, please.
+Please leave by 6 o'clock.

Negative

Use don't (not doesn't) for singular or plural.

− Don't use this desk.  Doesn't use this desk.
−Don't change desks.
−Don't use this desk, please.
−Please don't move these boxes.
