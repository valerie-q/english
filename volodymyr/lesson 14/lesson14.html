Exercise 1 — Spot the difference: "a few" vs "a little"

Here are a few tips to follow if you want to land the job of your dreams:

  1. Understanding whether you can do the job means being familiar with the technology, the company, the industry, and the position. It invariably involves some research — find a little time for it.
  2. Remember — there are many things you can be appreciated for. When you begin looking for the right organization, you should think about the experience you already have, including both basic technical experience and the specific projects where you applied it.
  3. Do not be shy if the interviewer is trying to get a little more information out of you. There are few hiring managers who will give you the job if you seem socially incompetent.
  4. To boost your confidence, think about the fact that most hiring managers were not trained as interviewers and have little if any experience in interviewing people. Usually, they see it as a distraction to the task at hand and don't spend much time preparing for it.

Exercise 2 — Countable or uncountable? 5 min

Quantifiers are words that describe quantity. We use different quantifiers depending on whether the word is countable or uncountable.

💡
Pro tip: if you can use the word in the plural form (e.g. person → people), it's countable.

Decide whether the words below are countable or uncountable. If countable, write the plural form.

  1. job →
  2. technology →
  3. company →
  4. industry →
  5. position →
  6. research →
  7. time →
  8. organization →
  9. feedback →
  10. project →
  11. manager →
  12. information →

CountableUncountable
Many (problems) / A lot ofMuch (work) / A lot of
Few = not enough — We have few people leftLittle = not enough — We have little money left
A few = a couple — I've used BitBucket a few timesA little = some amount — I have a little experience with that

Affirmative: Some — I have some experience in this field
Negative: Any — I don't have any issues with that

Some and Any can be used with both countable and uncountable nouns.

Пояснення

few / a few (з обчислюваними іменниками — problems, people, minutes...): few = мало, недостатньо (негативний відтінок); a few = кілька, трохи (нейтрально).

little / a little (з незлічуваними — time, money, experience...): little = мало, недостатньо; a little = трохи, певна кількість (нейтрально).

much / many: many — з обчислюваними, much — з незлічуваними; a lot of / lots of підходить для обох типів.

some / any: some — у стверджувальних реченнях; any — у заперечних. У питаннях "some" звучить, коли ти вже очікуєш ствердну відповідь, "any" — коли справді не знаєш.
Do you have some ideas?
Do you have any ideas?

💬
Teacher Tip
💡
Native speakers often make an intentional grammar mistake and use "less" with countable nouns instead of "fewer": less people, less problems, etc. This is acceptable in informal speech.
