  1. I have question. (a little/a quick)
  2. I have for you. (some feedback/feedbacks)
  3. The equipment (has been bought/were bought)
  4. This done! (can't be/can't to be)
  5. I don't have (much comment/many comments)
  6. She to participate again. (will be asked/will asked)
  7. We need to update our pricing information (little/a little)

Answers
  1. a quick
  2. some feedback
  3. has been bought
  4. can't be
  5. much comment*
  6. will be asked
  7. a little
  8. a few

*technically "much" pairs with the uncountable "comment" here — some versions of this exercise accept "many comments" too, discuss with your teacher.

The Story
01
Innovation vs. Reality
What Apple announced, and what testers found
📱
The announcement

On September 9, 2026, Apple unveiled the iPhone 18 Pro / Pro Max and its first-ever foldable device, the iPhone Duo, starting at $1,999. Folded, it's the size of a passport; unfolded, it has a 7.6-inch screen and IP68 water/dust resistance.

FactDetail
Display supplierThe foldable display is supplied by Samsung Display — a 3-year exclusive deal, reportedly around $250 per panel (one of the most expensive components in the device)
The hingeMade of titanium, built from over 100 individual components
The creaseDespite expectations, the screen crease is still visible — smaller than on Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold, but not eliminated
RepairabilityIndependent testers (iFixit) are already predicting a low repairability score, based on how foldables have performed in the past (some previous foldables scored as low as 4/10)

unveiled — представила, розкрила

foldable — складаний

exclusive deal — ексклюзивна угода

hinge — шарнір, завіса

crease — складка (на екрані)

repairability — ремонтопридатність

🔍
From a QA tester's perspective

Apple's own marketing video shows the hinge surviving 130 lbs of pressure without breaking. But independent YouTubers, like JerryRigEverything, run their own bend tests, scratch tests, and fold-cycle tests before trusting a company's claims.

Discussion
  1. Should companies be allowed to self-report durability stats, or should independent testing always be required?
  2. If you were the QA lead for this product, what edge cases would you want to test before launch? (dust in the hinge, cold weather folding, one-handed use, etc.)
  3. Apple buys its foldable screens from Samsung — its biggest rival in the phone market. Is that a smart business move or a risky one?
  4. Do you think "innovation" and "reliability" are always in conflict, or can a product have both?

Hardware & Testing Vocabulary
Terms from the launch and reviews
🔑
Key terms
TermMeaning
Creasea visible line/fold mark on a foldable screen
Hingethe mechanical joint that lets the phone fold
Durabilityhow well something withstands wear and damage over time
Exclusive supply dealan agreement where only one company provides a component
Variable aperturea camera feature that lets the lens opening change size (controls how much light enters)
Teardowntaking a device apart to inspect its components (usually for reviews)
Bend testa stress test where reviewers try to physically bend a device to check its durability
Repairabilityhow easy (or hard) a device is to fix or take apart
Fold-cycle (test)a durability test that repeatedly opens/closes a foldable device to simulate years of use

Hinge — шарнір, завіса

Teardown — розбирання пристрою (для огляду)

Bend test — тест на згин

Repairability — ремонтопридатність

Fold-cycle (test) — тест циклів згинання

2️⃣
Exercise B — Passive voice recap

Rewrite each sentence in the passive voice.

  1. Apple unveiled the iPhone Duo on September 9. →
  2. Samsung supplies the foldable display. →
  3. Many reviewers have already criticized the crease. →
  4. iFixit will test the phone's repairability next week. →
  5. Someone is currently running a fold-cycle test on the device. →
Answers
  1. The iPhone Duo was unveiled by Apple on September 9.
  2. The foldable display is supplied by Samsung.
  3. The crease has already been criticized by many reviewers.
  4. The phone's repairability will be tested by iFixit next week.
  5. A fold-cycle test is currently being run on the device.

How to Present a Product
Language from tech keynotes
🎤
Watch the opening — then look at the language

Notice how the speaker builds emotional connection before getting to any product details. Here are some of the actual phrases used, and what they're doing:

What it's doingPhrase from the speech
Opening warmly"It is great to be here."
Personal storytelling"I never could have imagined a moment like this."
Stating company values"We obsess over the details. We care enormously about design and simplicity."
Describing the ideal product"...incredibly powerful, but also simple and natural and intuitive."
Framing the mission"We just want to make things that have a meaningful impact in people's lives."
Building anticipation"There's still so much more ahead of us."
Transitioning to the main event"I'm excited. The teams are excited. Let's get into it."

obsess over the details — приділяти маніакальну увагу деталям

care enormously about — надзвичайно дбати про

have a meaningful impact — мати вагомий вплив

there's so much more ahead — попереду ще багато всього

Discussion
  1. Notice the speaker never mentions a single product feature in this whole clip — why do you think they start this way instead?
  2. Which phrase here sounds the most "corporate," and which sounds the most genuine to you?
  3. If you had to open a product pitch for your own team, would you copy this emotional style, or go straight to the facts? Why?
