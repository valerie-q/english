Article recap
Google
Building company culture, trust and connection for hybrid workers Natasha Tamiru · Think with Google · 2024

Hybrid working has profoundly reshaped our approach to work-life balance and wellbeing. While flexibility brings notable benefits, it also presents challenges — mental health, loneliness, and hampered career progression are increasingly familiar issues.

It's often a lack of trust that leads to mandates for full-time office returns and excessive employee monitoring. However, such measures risk alienating talent and jeopardising the psychological safety vital for business effectiveness.

At Google, we established clear rules of engagement — set office days, core meeting hours, and video call protocols. These build cognitive trust (competence and reliability) and affective trust (genuine interpersonal connection). Both are key drivers of psychological safety.

Teams can thrive in any setting — but culture has to be designed for. It won't materialise by itself.

📖
Ex. 1A — What is an elevator pitch? EfT p.27 5 min

What is an elevator pitch?

An elevator pitch is a quick overview of your background and skills, or a brief description of any products you want to sell. It is called so because it shouldn't last longer than a typical elevator ride — 30 seconds to 1 minute. After all, you don't want to bore your listeners, do you?

You will have to modify your elevator pitch to suit a particular purpose or occasion. When you introduce yourself to a colleague, your pitch is going to be shorter and less sales-y than when you are introducing yourself at a networking conference. However, it is useful to have a written main draft of your elevator pitch that you can easily edit based on your needs.

Where can you use an elevator pitch?

  • 📌 At job fairs and networking events
  • 📌 At interviews as an answer to "Tell me about yourself"
  • 📌 In your LinkedIn "About" section
  • 📌 On your website or blog
  • 📌 When you introduce yourself to a new client or team
🏗️
How to build a great elevator pitch 5 min

A good elevator pitch has 3 key steps. Read each one and the example below it.

1 Who you are and what you do

Start with your name and a clear, simple description of what you do. Avoid job titles alone — explain the impact of your work.

"My name's Stephan Taleb and I'm a full-stack web designer. I help startups create growth-driven products and brands."
2 Identify a problem you can solve and explain how you solve it

Describe the challenge your audience faces — then explain how you uniquely solve it. This is what makes a pitch memorable.

"In today's busy world, your customers are constantly bombarded with different marketing messages, which makes it significantly more difficult for customers to focus and make the right choice. In order to stand out, your signal should be clear and precise."
"I design memorable customer experiences that allow startups to communicate their message and value to their target audience by using cutting-edge web tools and my knowledge of business strategy."
3 Wrap up with a call to action

End with something that invites the next step — a meeting, a visit to your website, or simply an exchange of contacts.

"If you need an awesome custom-made website, you can book a free consultation on my website. I would love to hear from you!"
Useful phrases — mix and match
I work in… (online marketing / product development) general field
I work as a… at / for… (company name) role + company
I help… (Western companies scale their digital presence) impact-first intro
I take care of / I handle… (all product strategy) responsibilities
I oversee / I'm in charge of… (a cross-functional team) leadership role
I make sure that… (our product delivers real value) outcomes focus
My job is to… / I do… (full-stack development) clear role description
Right now I am… (developing / expanding into new markets) current focus

Grammar — Past Simple vs Present Perfect
10 min
📐
Grammar reference — forms & signals
Past Simple Present Perfect
Affirmative I worked on that project. I have worked on that project.
Negative She didn't build trust with her team. She hasn't built trust with her team.
Question Did you ever deal with a toxic person? Have you ever dealt with a toxic person?
When to use Completed action — time is known or finished: last year, in 2020, yesterday, last March Past action connected to now — time unknown or unfinished: since, for, already, just, ever, yet, recently
Signal words
Past Simple yesterday · last week/month/year · in 2020 · ago · when I was… · on Monday
Present Perfect already · just · yet · ever · never · since · for · recently · so far · this week/month/year

Exercise — correct the mistakes 5 min

Each sentence has a tense mistake. Find it and write the correct version.

  1. I have started this job in 2019.
  2. Did you ever work with someone you didn't trust?
  3. We didn't establish clear rules yet.
  4. She has left the company last April.
  5. I already told him about the budget last week.
  6. They have moved to a hybrid model in 2021.
Answer key
  1. I started this job in 2019. — specific year = Past Simple
  2. Have you ever worked with someone you didn't trust? — life experience = Present Perfect
  3. We haven't established clear rules yet. — yet = Present Perfect
  4. She left the company last April. — specific time = Past Simple
  5. I already told him about the budget last week. — last week = Past Simple
  6. They moved to a hybrid model in 2021. — specific year = Past Simple
✏️
Choose the correct form 7 min

Choose the correct tense and explain your choice.

  1. I (work) in product management for over 10 years.
    a) worked   b) have worked
  2. Our team (launch) the new app last March.
    a) launched   b) has launched
  3. you ever (deal) with a toxic team member?
    a) Did you ever deal   b) Have you ever dealt
  4. We (not establish) clear rules of engagement yet.
    a) didn't establish   b) haven't established
  5. Sinek (spend) time with the Navy SEALs and (learn) a lot about trust.
    a) spent / learned   b) has spent / has learned
  6. The company (move) to a hybrid model since 2021.
    a) moved   b) has moved
Answer key
  1. have worked — still ongoing, no specific time
  2. launched — specific time (last March)
  3. Have you ever dealt — life experience, no specific time
  4. haven't established — not done yet, relevant now
  5. spent / learned — completed event, specific past context
  6. has moved — change that started in past, still relevant
