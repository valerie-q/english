Grammar — Say vs Tell vs Speak vs Talk
💬
SAY

Meaning: to express a thought or words — what someone says, without focusing on who they say it to.

Used:

  • when quoting direct speech
  • before a reported clause, when there's no indirect object (no "me", "him", "her" right after)
  • with a direct object but no indirect object
Examples
"Plato is my friend, but truth is a better friend," said Aristotle.
He said he likes horror movies a lot.
Who said that?
Don't say a word. / She didn't say my name.
📢
TELL

Meaning: to inform, instruct, or relate something to a specific person.

Used:

  • with an indirect object — the person being told
  • when followed by an infinitive (tell + person + to + verb), it shifts toward meaning "order/instruct"
Examples
Our teacher told us an interesting story.
Tell me everything about yourself!
My coach told me what to do to win the contest.
My father told me to clean up the mess after the party.
Fixed expressions with "tell": tell a lie · tell the truth · tell a story · tell fibs / tell tall tales
🎙️
SPEAK

Meaning: to talk, usually in a more formal register — emphasizes the act of speaking itself, the language used, or addressing a group.

Used:

  • "speak to" (a person) / "speak about" (a topic)
  • to name which language someone uses
  • to describe giving a speech / addressing an audience
Examples
She speaks 4 languages.
Today I have spoken to him about a party.
What language is he speaking?
Did Adam speak at the meeting this morning?
Common collocations: speak fluently · speak quietly / softly · speak quickly / rapidly
🗣️
TALK

Meaning: very close to "speak" but more casual/conversational. There are situations where "speak" simply wouldn't fit.

Used: mostly in informal, everyday conversation — chatting, discussing, having a casual exchange.

Examples
Please stop talking! ("speak" wouldn't work here)
Did you talk to him yesterday?
What are you talking about?
Have you talked to her today? / My friend talks too much.
Worth remembering: "stop talking" — never "stop speaking" in this everyday sense.
📊
Quick comparison
Verb Used when… Example
say quoting direct speech, reported clauses without an indirect object He said he likes horror movies.
tell informing a specific person; giving instructions My coach told me what to do.
speak formal register; naming a language; addressing a group She speaks four languages.
talk informal, everyday conversation What are you talking about?
