The rule of three "whys"

The rule of 3 "whys"

The best way to tackle complex issues is to break them down step-by-step until you get to the root cause of the problem. Once you know what is actually causing the issue, the solution will be much easier to find!

One way you can go about breaking issues down is by asking the "why" question at least three times. For example:

Problem: I'm not getting enough sleep.

  • Question 1: Why am I not getting enough sleep?
    Answer: Because I always go to bed late.
  • Question 2: Why do I go to bed late?
    Answer: Because I stay up scrolling social media.
  • Question 3: Why do I stay up scrolling social media?
    Answer: Because that's how I unwind from a stressful day at work.

Solution: Replace evening social media time with a different relaxing activity: exercising, reading a book, learning a language and set a time limit for it (for instance, 15 minutes).

You can apply this technique to all areas of your life: work, relationships, personal development, etc.

Glossary of technical terms
▾
Lesson 3 terms — a quick reference
Encryption
A method of securing data by scrambling it so that only authorized parties can understand it by decrypting (unscrambling) it.
Supercomputer
An extremely powerful computer with superior computing power.
Virtual Reality
A computer-generated simulation in which a person can interact within an artificial three-dimensional environment using electronic devices, such as special goggles or gloves fitted with sensors.
Augmented Reality
A technology that superimposes a computer-generated image on a user's view of the real world, providing a composite view.
Artificial Intelligence
Computer systems that are able to perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as visual perception, speech recognition, decision-making, and translation between languages.
Early adopter
An individual or business who uses a new product, innovation, or technology before others.
Primary
In UI design, a "primary" color is a color that makes up most of the design, as opposed to the "accent" color used to highlight buttons and other calls-to-action.
Business forecasting
The process of predicting future developments in business based on analysis of trends in past and present data.
Gaming (the gaming industry)
The industry involved with the development, marketing, and monetization of video games.
Quick check: cover the definitions and try explaining each term out loud, in your own words, before you look.

Read: what's actually behind each trend

Technology keeps moving fast, and for businesses, keeping up isn't optional anymore. Based on insights from Gartner, here are 10 trends expected to shape how companies build products and compete in 2026.

01
AI-Native Platforms
Software built with AI at its core, not bolted on as an extra feature — using it to automate processes and support decision-making across the whole product.
02
Multi-Agent AI Systems
Several AI "agents" working together on one task, each handling a different part of the process, to complete complex jobs faster than a single model could.
03
Domain-Specific AI Models
AI trained specifically for one industry — finance, healthcare, retail — instead of a generic model, which makes its output far more accurate for that field.
04
Advanced AI Infrastructure
The computing power behind all this AI: scalable cloud systems, high-performance hardware, and modern data platforms built to handle heavy AI workloads.
05
Confidential Computing
A way of protecting data even while it's actively being processed, not just when it's stored — important for companies handling sensitive or regulated information.
06
AI Security Platforms
Tools built specifically to protect AI systems themselves: the models, the training data, and the automated decisions they make.
07
Intelligent Automation
Automation that goes beyond simple, rule-based tasks — using AI to handle more complex, judgment-based work and reduce manual effort.
08
Real-Time Data Analytics
Instead of waiting for end-of-day or end-of-week reports, companies analyze data as it comes in, so they can react to changes immediately.
09
Sustainable IT Infrastructure
Energy-efficient data centers and "greener" tech setups, driven by both environmental concerns and the desire to cut operating costs.
10
Integrated Digital Ecosystems
Different apps, tools, and data sources connected so they work together smoothly, instead of existing as separate, disconnected systems.

The common thread across all 10? Smarter systems, tighter security, and decisions made on real-time data. Companies that start adapting now will have the advantage later.
