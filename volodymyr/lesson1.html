POPULAR JOBS IN TECH

INTRODUCTION

The software industry is one of the fastest growing industries in the entire world, with new jobs appearing every year. Not only that, but a lot of the software jobs also don't require a university degree. Here are 5 popular new tech jobs you can learn to do from scratch.

01 · UX WRITER

A UX Writer helps create a great user experience through text. They are responsible for any text you read on landing pages, in contact forms, on buttons etc. Their texts must be clear, concise, and useful. You encounter the works of a UX writer every time you use an app or the internet. When they are doing their job well, you don't even notice it.

02 · DATA SCIENTIST

Many people say that data science is the most attractive career path of the 21st century. Data Scientists work directly with business stakeholders to help them understand and use data for strategic needs. They collect, store and analyze data and create algorithms and predictive models. In order to qualify for a data scientist role you need to have expertise in data modeling and be proficient in a range of programming languages.

03 · WEB DEVELOPER

Despite what some people might think, the demand for qualified web developers keeps growing with each year. Because web development is evolving so quickly, web developers have to keep up with the industry changes and learn how to compete on the job market. Some choose to learn UI/UX design. Others specialize in a specific niche such as platform development.

04 · DEVOPS ENGINEER

You will have to learn a broad range of technologies from scratch including programming languages, operating systems, cloud computing, networking, and continuous Integration. DevOps engineers help shorten development time, produce high-quality software and optimize communication between developers, testers and system administrators.

05 · BLOCKCHAIN DEVELOPER

Blockchain technology is still hugely popular these days. In a nutshell, a blockchain is a decentralized distributed ledger that helps perform various transactions. Businesses are excited to incorporate blockchain into their processes because it's a cost-efficient, transparent way of managing transactions. As a blockchain developer, you will need a solid knowledge of data structures and blockchain architecture.

Ex. 1A — What tenses are used in the highlighted sentences? EfT p.17 4 min

Read the text. Identify the tense of each highlighted sentence.

If you want to create an app, you need to perform market research first. Who is your audience? Where do they live? What languages do they speak? What devices do they use? How much money do they spend? In the U.S. an average iOS user spends roughly twice as much as an average Android user does.

Try to imagine your ideal user. Visualize them. Where are they? What are they doing at the moment? Where do they work? How do they spend their free time? What are they dreaming about?

Which tense is used in the blue sentences — and why?

Answer
  1. Blue sentences 1 & 2 → Present Simple — facts and general truths
  2. Blue sentences 3 & 4 → Present Continuous — actions happening at a specific moment (imagining the user right now)
📐
Grammar reference
Present Simple vs Present Continuous
Present Simple Facts, habits, routines → I work in digital marketing. She manages a team.
Present Continuous Happening now, temporary → I'm currently building a new app. We're expanding to the US.
⚠️ Stative verbs know, understand, own, want, like, need, believe — never use in Continuous

Ex. 4A — Choose the correct form EfT p.22 8 min

Choose the correct option and explain your choice out loud.

  1. Harry (work) from home this week due to health problems.
    a) work   b) works   c) is working   d) are working
  2. What (the clients / want)?
    a) What does the clients want?   b) What are the clients wanting?   c) What are the clients want?   d) What do the clients want?
  3. We usually (discuss) these issues over Skype every Thursday morning.
    a) discuss   b) discusses   c) is discussing   d) are discussing
  4. Dwight (learn) another programming language in order to find a new job.
    a) learn   b) learns   c) is learning   d) are learning
  5. For some reason, her colleagues (not like) her.
    a) don't like   b) doesn't like   c) are not liking   d) is not liking
  6. (Charlie / still / fix) these bugs?
    a) Do Charlie still fix?   b) Does Charlie still fix?   c) Is Charlie still fixing?   d) Are Charlie still fixing?
Answer key + reason
  1. is working — temporary this week (Continuous)
  2. What do the clients want? — "want" = stative verb
  3. discuss — regular action every Thursday (Simple)
  4. is learning — temporary ongoing action (Continuous)
  5. don't like — "like" = stative verb
  6. Does Charlie still fix — he/she/it → does (Simple)

What is an elevator pitch? EfT p.25–26 3 min

An elevator pitch is a short, clear, engaging intro about who you are and what you do — short enough to deliver in a 30-second elevator ride.

3 steps of a good elevator pitch
1. Who you are and what you do "My name's [name]. I run an online marketing agency."
2. The problem you solve "I help Western companies scale their digital presence in new markets."
3. Wrap up with a call to action "If you're looking to grow your app's user base, I'd love to connect."
