Reading — Building Trust in Hybrid Teams Think with Google · 2024 7 min
Google
Building company culture, trust and connection for hybrid workers Natasha Tamiru · Think with Google · February 2024

Hybrid working has profoundly reshaped our approach to work-life balance and wellbeing. The shift towards flexibility has brought notable benefits, such as increased productivity and access to global talent. But it also presents challenges — mental health, loneliness, and hampered career progression are increasingly familiar issues.

While the benefits of flexible working are far too great to return to the ways of old, it's become apparent that there's no one-size-fits-all approach. Instead, we must thoughtfully design our workspaces to address the needs of both organisations and the individuals who work for them.

For organisations and leadership, a core challenge of the shift towards flexible work is that they've had to rethink how they manage teams and measure their performance. It's often a lack of trust — whether in employees or the process itself — that leads to mandates for full-time office returns and excessive employee monitoring. However, such measures risk alienating talent and jeopardising the psychological safety vital for business effectiveness.

At Google, we've made hybrid work by establishing clear rules of engagement — set office days, core hours for meetings, and protocols for video calls. These rules help build cognitive trust (clear expectations around job competence and results) but also affective trust (stronger interpersonal relationships within teams). Both are key drivers of psychological safety.

Teams and individuals can thrive in any setting as long as organisations foster a strong, positive culture led by intentional efforts and shared values. But that won't materialise by itself. It has to be designed for and built into every aspect of your workspace.

After reading

Grammar — Past Simple vs Present Perfect
10 min
📐
Grammar reference — from the video
Past Simple vs Present Perfect
Past Simple completed action at a specific time → "I spent time with the Navy SEALs." / "In 2016, Apple opened their API."
Present Perfect past action with present relevance, or experience → "I have spent over a decade leading programmes." / "Have you ever tried to dictate an email?"
⚠️ Key difference Past Simple = when? is clear or important. Present Perfect = when? doesn't matter, or the result matters now.
✏️
Choose the correct form 7 min

Choose the correct tense and explain your choice.

  1. I ______ (work) in product management for over 10 years.
    a) worked   b) have worked
  2. Our team ______ (launch) the new app last March.
    a) launched   b) has launched
  3. ______ you ever ______ (deal) with a toxic team member?
    a) Did you ever deal   b) Have you ever dealt
  4. We ______ (not establish) clear rules of engagement yet.
    a) didn't establish   b) haven't established
  5. Sinek ______ (spend) time with the Navy SEALs and ______ (learn) a lot about trust.
    a) spent / learned   b) has spent / has learned
  6. The company ______ (move) to a hybrid model since 2021.
    a) moved   b) has moved
Answer key
  1. have worked — still ongoing, no specific time
  2. launched — specific time (last March)
  3. Have you ever dealt — life experience, no specific time
  4. haven't established — not done yet, relevant now
  5. spent / learned — completed event, specific past context
  6. has moved — change that started in past, still relevant
