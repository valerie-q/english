Tell me three statements about yourself — two true, one false. I have to guess which is the lie. Then we swap.

The twist: all three statements must be about your professional life — your job, colleagues, clients, work habits, career, business travel, meetings, etc.

Revision B — Find the wrong phrase 1.3 Ex 2B 5 min

Each mini-dialogue has one awkward or wrong phrase. Find it and suggest a better alternative.

  1. A: Hi! Good to finally meet you in person — I've seen you so many times on video calls.
    B: "Likewise. You look different to what I imagined. Anyway, let's get straight to the agenda."
  2. A: Let me introduce you to our Head of Operations — have you met before?

📐
Grammar reference — Present Simple and Continuous BP p.118

Present Simple — when to use

We can use the Present Simple to talk about:

  • Permanent situations He supervises the production line. He works in the finance department.
  • General facts Water freezes at 0 degrees. It's one of the biggest companies in the world.
  • Repeated / regular actions They work in an office. She deals with customers.

Present Continuous — when to use

We can use the Present Continuous to talk about:

  • Things happening at the same time as we are speaking or writing We're waiting for a delivery. He's showing Mr Jones around the factory.
  • Temporary situations I'm staying with a friend while I'm in London this week. He's acting as Head of Finance while his boss is on maternity leave.
  • Future plans/arrangements We're meeting the clients tomorrow morning. She is visiting our offices next week.

Verbs rarely used in the continuous

These verbs are rarely used in the Present Continuous:

be, believe, contain, dislike, hate, hear, know, like, love, need, own, possess, smell, sound, understand

Verbs that change meaning

Some common verbs change their meaning in Present Simple vs Present Continuous:

  • think I think you're going to find it very interesting. (= my opinion) I'm thinking about the induction day. (= the topic in my mind)
  • have I have a good car. (= own / possess) He's having breakfast. (= eating)
  • see I see your point. (= understand) I'm seeing him tomorrow. (= meeting)
⚠️
Common mistake: "I'm knowing him" → WRONG. Know is a stative verb — always use simple: "I know him."

Exercise 3B — Write the reply BP p.16 8 min

Write a reply to Greg's email in around 80 words. Thank him and confirm which day you can attend and why you cannot attend on the other day. Work on it section by section.

Stage 1 — Opening (2–3 lines)

Use a greeting + reason for writing. Try: "Thank you for your email about…" or "Further to your email, I…"

Stage 2 — Main information (3–4 lines)

Explain which day you can't attend and why. Then confirm which day works. Use: "Unfortunately, I'm not able to… because…" and "I would be happy to attend on…"

Stage 3 — Additional detail (1–2 lines)

Mention the possible late arrival on Friday if relevant. Use: "Please note that I may be slightly late as…"

Stage 4 — Closing (1 line)

Use an appropriate closing phrase from Exercise 2B. Match the level of formality.

Model answer — reveal after writing
Dear Greg,

Thank you for your email about the induction day. Unfortunately, I'm not able to attend on Thursday as I have a dentist's appointment at 8.30 a.m. and I'm also going to a conference that day.

I would be happy to attend on Friday instead. However, please note that I may be slightly late arriving in the morning.

I look forward to meeting everyone. Kind regards,
Jill
